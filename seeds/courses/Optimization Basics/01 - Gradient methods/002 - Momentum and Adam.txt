Momentum keeps an exponential moving average of past gradients and steps along that average. It smooths the zig-zag of SGD in narrow valleys and accelerates along consistent directions. The usual coefficient is 0.9.

Adam keeps two moving averages: the mean of the gradient (first moment, beta1 = 0.9) and the mean of the squared gradient (second moment, beta2 = 0.999). Each parameter is updated by the first moment divided by the square root of the second, so parameters with noisy gradients take smaller steps.

AdamW decouples weight decay from the gradient update. Instead of adding L2 regularisation to the loss, it shrinks the weights directly each step. AdamW is the default optimiser for transformers.

Bias correction divides both moments by one minus beta to the power t, which matters in the first few hundred steps when the averages are still near zero.
