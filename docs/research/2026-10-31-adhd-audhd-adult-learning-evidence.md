# How ADHD and AuDHD Adults Learn: An Evidence Review for Designing an AuDHD-Centred Adaptive Tutor

For an adult AuDHD learner, the strongest design evidence points to a tutor that **plans for day-to-day variability rather than average performance, uses retrieval practice with external scaffolding (because ADHD learners benefit from testing as much as others but under-use it on their own), offloads time and memory onto the interface, and avoids shame-laden mechanics like streaks and hard timers**. Much of the rest of the popular "ADHD/AuDHD learning" canon (hyperfocus as a superpower, RSD, dopamine deficiency, monotropism, body doubling) is either lived-experience-led, supported only by small studies, or extrapolated from children. Research that looks specifically at AuDHD adults learning is close to nonexistent.

## TL;DR

- **Robust, design-ready evidence (meta-analyses, controlled trials):** increased intra-individual variability in ADHD performance; timing/time-perception deficits; steeper delay discounting; an intact retrieval-practice (testing) effect in ADHD college students; small benefits from white/pink noise for ADHD (it impairs non-ADHD people); moderate acute-exercise effects on inhibitory control in adults; and stimulants improving productivity and on-task behaviour without clearly improving learning of content (in children).
- **AuDHD is common but understudied:** roughly 38–40% of autistic people meet criteria for ADHD (Rong et al., 2021 meta-analysis of 63 studies). Executive-function profiles in co-occurring groups look more like ADHD-alone and are more impaired than autism-alone, but these data are mostly from children and that specific comparison rests on very few studies. Monotropism, autistic inertia and autistic burnout are well-articulated, community-led constructs backed by qualitative and early psychometric work, not by intervention trials.
- **Design stance:** build a "variability-first" tutor. That means energy/mode check-ins, scaffolded retrieval to a criterion, short chunked sessions with soft (not hard) timers, explicit predictable structure, a capture "parking lot", optional body-double presence, movement primers, optional noise, and no streaks or loss-framed progress. Treat every unvalidated folk mechanism as an A/B experiment on yourself, not a law.

## Evidence-strength legend

- **A** = meta-analysis or randomised/controlled trial(s) with reasonable samples
- **B** = small controlled or cross-sectional studies, psychometric development, single experiments
- **C** = qualitative studies, expert/practitioner consensus, standards bodies
- **D** = community/lived-experience reports, social-media framing, commercial claims
- **[child→adult]** = finding mainly from children/adolescents, extrapolated to adults

## (a) Executive summary: the 13 most robust, design-relevant findings

1. **Inconsistency is the signature, not the exception (A).** A meta-analysis of 319 studies (Kofler et al., 2013) found greater reaction-time variability in children/adolescents (Hedges' g = 0.76) and adults (g = 0.46) with ADHD than in typically developing groups. Stimulants attenuated it (g = −0.74).\[1\] Increased variability was present in roughly 30% of adults with ADHD, not all.\[2\] It was also not specific to ADHD: adults with ADHD were indistinguishable from clinical control groups.\[3\] Design: never infer mastery or failure from one session.
2. **Retrieval practice works in ADHD, but ADHD learners don't deploy it spontaneously (A/B).** College students with ADHD showed a moderate testing effect equal to controls (ds = 0.50 vs 0.57; Knouse et al., 2016).\[4\] A 36-vs-36 study using Swahili-English word pairs (Serrano et al., Frontiers in Psychology, 2023) found that "all participants showed a benefit of retrieval practice relative to restudy and participant status did not moderate the effect", though unmedicated ADHD students performed worse overall and used fewer deep encoding strategies. In self-regulated study, ADHD students used retrieval practice less and dropped items after fewer than one correct recall (Knouse et al., 2020).\[5\] Design: system-driven retrieval to a criterion.
3. **Free recall of whole texts is the weak spot (B).** One study found no testing benefit for ADHD students on free recall of text (Dudukovic et al., 2015). A 2023 replication with section-level recall found no testing effect in either group.\[6\] Design: prefer cued, item-level retrieval over "summarise the whole chapter".
4. **Time perception is measurably altered (A, partly [child→adult]).** A JAACAP meta-analysis of 55 studies found deficits across time discrimination, estimation, production and reproduction (Marx et al., 2021).\[7\]\[8\] A lifespan meta-analysis of 824 effect sizes found a mean g = 0.688, moderated by age and working memory (Metcalfe et al., 2023).\[9\] The adult-specific literature is mixed, and Mette's 2023 review in IJERPH concluded that "the number of studies on time perception in adult ADHD is very scarce". Design: externalise time visually.
5. **Steeper delay discounting (A).** Case-control meta-analysis (Jackson & MacKillop, 2016; 21 studies, N = 3,913): people with ADHD prefer smaller-sooner rewards, with a medium effect.\[10\]\[11\] Design: make payoff immediate and visible inside each session.
6. **Acute exercise primes inhibitory control in adults (A, small evidence base).** A 2026 meta-analysis in adults with ADHD (Xu, Zhao & Hu; 14 studies reviewed, 8 meta-analysed) found acute exercise had a moderate effect on inhibitory control (g = 0.55) and a small effect on core symptoms (g = 0.23).\[12\]\[13\] Design: offer a movement primer before demanding blocks.
7. **White/pink noise gives a small benefit for ADHD, and harms others (A, mostly youth/young adults).** JAACAP meta-analysis (Nigg et al., 2024; 13 studies, 335 participants): a small benefit on lab attention tasks for ADHD/high-symptom individuals, but impaired performance in non-ADHD individuals.\[14\] No brown-noise studies existed.\[15\] Design: optional, user-controlled, off by default.
8. **Stimulants raise productivity more than learning (A [child→adult]).** In a randomised crossover trial with 173 children (Pelham et al., 2022), medication increased arithmetic seatwork productivity (37% more problems per minute) and cut classroom rule violations (53% fewer per hour), but had "no detectable effect" on learning the curriculum content taught.\[16\] In healthy adults, stimulant cognitive effects are "probably modest overall" (Ilieva, Hook & Farah, 2015).\[17\] Design: log medication timing as context, but never let "on-med throughput" stand in for retention.
9. **AuDHD is common and has a heavier load (A for prevalence; B for profile).** Pooled ADHD prevalence in autism is 38.5% current and 40.2% lifetime (Rong et al., 2021).\[18\] In children, a 2024 meta-analysis of 36 studies (Ceruti et al., Children) reported that co-occurring groups' EF profiles resemble ADHD-alone and are more impaired than autism-alone, though that comparison rests on a single included study. In a clinic sample of 846 children and adolescents (Crosbie, Schachar et al., 2023), "ADHD traits accounted for neurocognitive impairment in ASD, but not vice versa". In an Irish adult ADHD clinic (N = 165; 2025), 44.8% had autistic traits on the AQ-10, and over about a year they had worse clinical outcomes, quality of life, social skills and family functioning, though not worse overall functionality.
10. **If-then plans improve inhibition in ADHD (A/B, [child→adult]).** Children with ADHD who added an implementation intention ("If I hear a tone, I will not press any key!") to a Go/No-Go goal improved inhibition to the level of non-ADHD peers (Gawrilow & Gollwitzer, 2008; N = 58 boys). Implementation intentions plus medication worked best.\[19\]\[20\] If-then plans also improved delay of gratification (Gawrilow et al., 2011; 45 inpatient + 47 outpatient children with ADHD).\[21\] No adult ADHD trials were found.
11. **Autistic inertia is real to those who live it, and is broken from outside (C).** First-hand accounts from 32 autistic adults described difficulty acting on intentions as pervasive, summed up in the title "No way out except from external intervention" (Buckle et al., 2021).\[22\] A 24-adult interview study found inertia is also enabling (sustained motion once started) and context-dependent.\[23\]\[24\]
12. **Autistic burnout is defined, and masking is its main driver (C).** Raymaker et al. (2020): "pervasive, long-term (typically 3+ months) exhaustion, loss of function, and reduced tolerance to stimulus", arising from "chronic life stress and a mismatch of expectations and abilities without adequate supports". Masking was "by far the most prominent life stressor".\[25\] Design: the tutor must detect sustained decline and shrink expectations rather than push.
13. **Accessibility standards converge on predictability, focus protection and no forced timing (C).** W3C COGA (2021) objectives include "Help Users Focus", "Ensure Processes Do Not Rely on Memory", and avoiding "timeouts" ("I need time to complete my work"), plus consistent visual design because "many users take a long time to learn new designs".\[26\]\[27\]\[28\]

## (b) Findings by area, with evidence ratings

### 1. Cognitive profile of ADHD adults relevant to learning

| Trait | What the evidence says | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Intra-individual variability | Greater RT variability in adults (g = 0.46) and children (g = 0.76). Medication reduces it. Not ADHD-specific versus other clinical groups. Ex-Gaussian meta-analyses show larger inter-trial variability without clear differences in speed.\[1\]\[29\] | A |
| Working memory & encoding | Retrieval itself appears intact. Deficits are more at encoding: unmedicated students used fewer deep strategies, and older RAVLT work points to executive retrieval errors (intrusions, double recalls) rather than storage loss.\[30\]\[31\] | B |
| Time perception / "time blindness" | Meta-analytic deficits across paradigms. Signal is strongest in children and short intervals; adult studies are scarce and mixed. "Time blindness" is a descriptor, not a diagnosis.\[11\]\[32\] | A [child→adult] / D for the label |
| Delay discounting / reward | Steeper discounting, medium effect. Some evidence that, in young people, steep discounting appears mainly with comorbid substance misuse.\[33\] Dopamine models are plausible mechanisms, not settled causes. | A (behaviour); B/C (mechanism) |
| Inhibition, planning, switching | EF deficits are well documented. Inhibition responds acutely to exercise and to if-then plans (in children). | A/B |
| Hyperfocus | Self-reported hyperfocus correlates with ADHD symptoms (Hupfeld et al., 2019; pilot n = 251, replication n = 372 with 162 high-symptom adults). But a matched case-control study of 78 diagnosed adults found **no difference** in occurrence, frequency, duration or pervasiveness, and *less* hyperfocus in educational settings (Groen et al., 2020). A qualitative study of 7 adults (Bowen & Horlin, 2026) describes it as "absorbed, trapped, or lost". Not a DSM criterion; poorly defined (Ashinoff & Abu-Akel, 2021).\[34\]\[35\]\[36\]\[37\] | B/C; contested |
| Emotional dysregulation | Widely documented in adult ADHD and clinically important. "RSD" as a construct is not established (see list d).\[38\] | B (dysregulation) / D (RSD) |
| Interest-based attention ("interest-based nervous system") | Practitioner framing (Dodson). Consistent with gamified-task data where people with more ADHD symptoms and higher reward responsiveness benefited most from game elements, but there is no direct validation of the construct.\[39\] | C/D |
| Sleep/energy variability, mind-wandering, processing speed | Not directly reviewed here. Treat as plausible contributors to variability, to be tracked by the system rather than assumed. | Not rated (gap in this review) |

### 2. Autistic and AuDHD adults

- **Monotropism (B/C).** Monotropism is a community-originated theory: attention concentrated on few interests at a time. The Monotropism Questionnaire (Garau et al., 2023; 47 items; online sample of 1,110, 756 autistic) found autism and ADHD "independently and jointly associated with higher self-report monotropism", with AuDHD scoring highest. It is still a preprint with a single validation sample, no test-retest data and no independent replication.\[40\]\[41\]\[42\]\[43\] Use it as a design lens (minimise forced switching, protect flow), not as established fact.
- **Autistic inertia (C).** Two qualitative studies (32 and 24 adults) describe difficulty both starting and stopping. External prompts are the most reported way to break it. The construct may overlap with executive dysfunction and ADHD "paralysis"; it is unresolved whether it is uniquely autistic.\[23\]\[44\]\[45\]
- **Autistic burnout and masking (C).** Raymaker et al. (2020) defined burnout from 19 interviews plus online sources (a small convenience sample). Causes: life stressors (masking, expectations, life changes) causing cumulative load, plus barriers to relief, until "expectations outweigh ability". Recovery came from "acceptance and social support, time off/reduced expectations, and doing things in an autistic way/unmasking". Burnout is distinct from depression and occupational burnout.\[25\]
- **Rejection sensitivity in autistic adults (C/D).** A 2026 scoping review (van Asselt, Reekers & Roke) mapped evidence on responses to rejection and criticism in autistic adults, prompted by social-media use of "RSD". The evidence base is fragmented.\[46\]
- **Detail-focused processing, predictive processing / intolerance of uncertainty, sensory differences, literal instruction, alexithymia/interoception (not re-verified here).** These are established areas in autism research, but this review did not retrieve adult learning-specific trials for them. Design use: explicit rules, unambiguous wording, predictable structure, user-controlled sensory settings, and energy check-ins that do not rely solely on introspective emotion labels (offer behavioural proxies instead).
- **AuDHD interaction (B, [child→adult]).** Evidence suggests additive or greater EF difficulty and worse longitudinal outcomes. Children with both conditions may respond less well to stimulants and have more adverse effects.\[47\]\[48\] The trait "conflict" (novelty-seeking vs sameness, impulsivity vs rigidity) is almost entirely lived-experience-described (D); no adult experimental studies were found. AuDHD also appears under-identified in adults: a 2025 BMC Health Services Research analysis of 2022 US commercial insurance claims (about 1.9 million adults) found only 1.7% of adults with ADHD had a co-existing autism diagnosis, far below the 16% previously reported by Pehlivanidis et al.

### 3. What learning science says works

| Technique | ADHD/AuDHD-specific evidence | Strength | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retrieval practice | Equal benefit in ADHD college students (several studies). Under-used when self-regulated. Weak for whole-text free recall.\[5\]\[6\]\[49\] | A/B | Use cued recall to criterion, driven by the system |
| Spacing | General learning-science consensus. No ADHD-specific spacing trials found. | General A / ADHD gap | Spacing pairs naturally with variable attendance |
| Interleaving vs blocking | No ADHD-specific evidence found. | Gap | Run as a personal experiment; mind the switching cost for monotropic learners |
| Chunking / single-task design | Supported by WM limits and by COGA "Avoid Too Much Content", "Make Short Critical Paths".\[28\] | B/C | |
| Explicit instruction, worked examples, cognitive load | General evidence is strong. Neurodivergent-specific adult evidence is thin. Autistic preference for explicit rules is consensus-level. | General A / C | Default to explicit, then fade to problem-solving |
| Multiple representations vs "learning styles" | Matching instruction to a preferred "style" lacks support (general literature). Multiple representations for a concept are a different, defensible idea. | General A against styles | |
| Immediate feedback | Fits steep delay discounting. Trials of feedback timing in ADHD adults were not found. | B (inferred) | |
| Metacognition/calibration | One RAVLT-type finding: adults with ADHD were as accurate as controls at predicting memory performance despite remembering fewer words.\[4\] | B | Confidence ratings are usable signals |
| If-then plans | Improve inhibition and delay of gratification in children with ADHD.\[21\] | A/B [child→adult] | |
| Gamification | A gamified CPT (94 adults) helped those with more ADHD symptoms and reward responsiveness most.\[39\] Retention-boost figures in app marketing are unverified.\[50\] | B / D | Engagement ≠ retention |

A 2026 roadmap paper notes "very few empirical studies", and earlier work noted discomfort with social presence.\[51\]\[52\]\[53\]\[54\] | B/C | Make presence optional and adjustable |
| Acute exercise | Moderate effect on inhibitory control (g = 0.55) in adults; fMRI study showed improved attention and processing speed after aerobic exercise.\[12\]\[55\] | A (small k) | |

| Timers / Pomodoro | No ADHD-specific trials found. COGA warns against timeouts. Time-perception deficits argue for visible time, not punitive deadlines.\[27\] | C | Soft timers that inform rather than cut off |
| Capture / "parking lot" | Practitioner consensus. Consistent with COGA "do not rely on memory".\[26\] | C | |
| Interest-first sequencing | Special interests as learning engines and monotropic flow are well described qualitatively; no comparative trials. | C/D | |

### 4. Barriers and failure modes

- **Streaks, loss-framing and all-or-nothing (D).** This is consistently reported in community and app-design commentary: a broken streak triggers abandonment rather than restart.\[57\] There are no controlled studies in ADHD adults, but it is coherent with emotional dysregulation and burnout models.\[50\]\[58\]\[59\]
- **Self-regulation failures, not ability failures (B).** The retrieval data show ADHD students benefit equally but stop practising too early.\[5\] Tools that require the learner to self-schedule will fail.
- **Overload and inconsistent UI (C).** COGA's emphasis on consistent, familiar design and limiting interruptions reflects the access needs of people with cognitive disabilities.\[26\]\[60\]
- **Burnout (C).** Cumulative load plus inability to rest is the main pathway.\[25\] Pressure-tracking features add load.
- **Medication (A [child→adult]).** Stimulants reduce variability and boost productivity. Effects on learning of content are not established, and effects in AuDHD may be weaker.\[1\]\[47\]\[61\]\[62\]
- **Cognitive disengagement syndrome / sluggish cognitive tempo.** Not reviewed in depth here (gap).

### 5. Technology-mediated learning

- **LLM tutors (C/D).** No controlled trials of LLM tutoring for ADHD or autistic adults were found. A Reddit-based analysis (Carik et al., 2025) found ADHD users mainly use LLMs for productivity (26.33% of discussions), report "prompting frustrations" as their main challenge (52%), and are highly concerned about false information (80%). Autistic users mainly used LLMs for interpersonal communication (34.67%).\[63\]\[64\] Design: zero-prompt workflows and visible source grounding.
- **Adaptive systems, open learner models, MOOCs for ADHD adults.** No relevant outcome studies were retrieved in this review (gap).
- **Accessibility guidance (C).** W3C COGA (2021 Working Group Note, supplemental to WCAG) has eight objectives: understand what things are, find what you need, clear content, avoid/correct mistakes, help users focus, don't rely on memory, provide help, support adaptation/personalisation.\[65\]\[66\]
- **Assistive tech for ADHD adults (C).** A 2026 scoping review on assistive technologies for adults with ADHD is in progress (preliminary results only).\[67\]

### 6. Strengths

- **Creativity/divergent thinking (B, contested).** In the general population, more ADHD symptoms are associated with higher divergent thinking (fluency, flexibility, originality) but not convergent thinking. Most studies find increased divergent thinking in subclinical high-trait people, not in those with the clinical disorder (Hoogman et al., 2020 review). An earlier meta-analysis found a negative association (Paek et al., 2016).\[68\]\[69\]
- **Hyperfocus/special interests (B/C).** Deep absorption in interests is real for many, but it is unreliable, hard to stop and less likely in educational contexts.\[35\]\[70\] Harness it by making the curriculum route through interests, not by expecting hyperfocus on demand.
- **Systemising/pattern recognition in autism (not re-verified here).** Plausibly relevant to programming/ML; treat as a hypothesis for this learner.

## (c) Findings to design implications

| Finding (strength) | AuDHS-Tutor design implication |
|---|---|
| High intra-individual variability (A) | Daily "energy/mode" check-in (e.g., Low / Steady / Surge) selects session type. Use rolling multi-session estimates for mastery. Never reset or penalise after bad days. |
| Retrieval works but is under-used (A/B) | The system schedules and launches retrieval. Each item is practised to a criterion of ≥2–3 correct recalls before being dropped. Use cued recall (code-completion, flashcards, "predict the output"), not whole-text summaries. |
| Spacing (general A) | Spaced review queue that tolerates gaps: overdue items are re-prioritised, not piled up as a guilt-inducing backlog. Cap daily reviews. |
| Time-perception deficits (A) | Visual elapsed/remaining time bars. Soft timers that chime and offer "continue / wrap up / park" instead of cutting off. Estimate-vs-actual feedback to calibrate planning. |
| Delay discounting (A) | Micro-payoffs within each session: immediate correctness feedback, a visible "what I can now do" artefact (working code, a chord change, a sentence). |
| Acute exercise (A) | Optional 5–10 minute movement primer before high-demand AI/ML or programming blocks. The movement block doubles as a primer. |
| White/pink noise (A, youth) | Built-in optional white/pink noise, user-toggled, off by default. Log the condition so the system can learn personally whether it helps. |
| Body doubling (B/C) | Optional ambient presence modes: silent AI "co-worker" status, a "study-with-me" recording, or a scheduled human session. The learner controls social intensity. |
| If-then plans (A/B child) | At session end, prompt one implementation intention: "If it's 7pm and I've eaten, then I open the guitar block." Surface it as the next-start cue. |
| Autistic inertia (C) | One-tap "start" with the first step already loaded (cursor in the exercise). Transitions announced in advance with countdowns. A "finish this thought" grace period before switching. |
| Monotropism (B/C) | Minimise forced task-switching within a session. Bundle tangents into a parking lot. Allow long sessions when the learner is in flow, with gentle body-need reminders. |
| Explicit rules / literal instruction (C) | Explicit learning objectives, success criteria and unambiguous wording. Worked example, then faded example, then independent problem. Socratic mode only on request. |
| Burnout and masking (C) | Detect sustained decline (falling check-ins, missed sessions). Automatically offer "maintenance mode" (reviews only, reduced load). No guilt messaging. |
| Shame/emotional dysregulation (B/D) | Neutral, specific, non-evaluative error feedback. No red-X walls. Frame errors as data. No public or social comparison. |
| Streak/abandonment risk (D) | No streaks. Show cumulative progress ("concepts you can now use"), which cannot be lost. Re-entry screen after absence: "Welcome back, here are 3 easy wins." |
| COGA consistency and focus (C) | Stable layout across all blocks (AI/ML, language, guitar, movement). Notifications off by default. Minimal choices per screen. Breadcrumbs showing step x of n. |
| Stimulant effects (A child) | Optional medication-timing log; schedule demanding new material inside personal effective windows if desired. Measure retention separately from throughput. |
| Calibration intact (B) | Ask for confidence ratings before reveal; use them to set review intervals and show calibration trends. |
| Interest-first (C/D) | Let the learner enter a topic via an interest-driven project, then back-fill prerequisites "just in time" with explicit links. |

## (d) Common claims that are NOT well supported or are contested

1. **"Hyperfocus is an ADHD superpower."** It is self-report correlated with ADHD traits, but one matched case-control study found no difference in diagnosed adults and less hyperfocus in educational activities. It is not a diagnostic criterion, is poorly defined, and often involves inability to disengage.\[36\]\[37\]
2. **"RSD is a diagnosis."** It is not in DSM-5. One 2026 scoping review reportedly concluded RSD "is not an established research construct", with no intervention trials. Supporting studies are qualitative (e.g., 5 undergraduates; 8 women) or case series.\[38\]\[71\] Emotional dysregulation is the better-supported construct.
3. **"ADHD is dopamine deficiency."** Reward-processing differences and steep discounting are robust at the behavioural level, but simple deficiency models are mechanistic simplifications. Steep discounting is also transdiagnostic (seen in depression, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia and SUD).\[72\]
4. **"Learning styles" (visual/auditory/kinaesthetic matching).** Not supported by the general learning-science literature. Multiple representations are fine; matching to a "style" is not evidence-based.
5. **"Brown noise helps ADHD focus."** No studies existed at the time of the 2024 meta-analysis. White/pink noise effects are small and lab-based.\[15\]\[73\]
6. **"Body doubling is proven."** Evidence consists of one 12-person VR preprint, survey/self-report work and conceptual frameworks.\[51\]\[52\]
7. **"Stimulants make you learn more."** In a child RCT they increased productivity but not curriculum learning. Healthy-adult effects are modest.\[17\]\[61\]
8. **"ADHD people are more creative."** Mixed. Gains are seen mainly in subclinical high-trait samples and in divergent (not convergent) thinking, and one meta-analysis found a negative association.\[68\]
9. **"Gamification boosts ADHD retention by X%."** Figures circulating on app vendor blogs could not be traced to verifiable studies.\[74\] Treat them as marketing.\[50\]
10. **"Monotropism is the proven theory of autism."** The flagship questionnaire is an unreplicated preprint.\[42\] The theory is valuable but not established.
11. **"Time blindness means ADHD people can't perceive time."** Timing is less accurate and more variable, especially in children, but adult findings are mixed.\[75\]

## (e) Open questions and gaps, especially for AuDHD adults

- No experimental learning studies (spacing, interleaving, feedback timing, worked examples) specifically in AuDHD adults were found. Nearly all co-occurrence EF data are from children.
- Retrieval-practice studies are confined to college students, short retention intervals and verbal materials. Nothing covers programming, motor skills (guitar) or language acquisition in ADHD adults.
- There are no controlled trials of LLM tutors, adaptive systems or open learner models for ADHD or autistic adults.
- Implementation-intention evidence is child-only (mostly boys in German clinical samples).\[20\]
- Monotropism, inertia and burnout need longitudinal, quantitative and intervention research. Their overlap with ADHD executive dysfunction is unresolved.
- How novelty-seeking (ADHD) and sameness-seeking (autism) trade off in learning-environment preferences is unstudied. This is the core AuDHD design tension.
- The ecological validity of noise, exercise and body-doubling effects beyond lab tasks and short sessions is unknown.
- The effects of streaks/gamification on long-term retention and dropout in ADHD adults are untested.

## Recommendations

1. **Build the scheduler around variability.** Use a daily energy/mode check-in, a review queue that tolerates gaps, and mastery estimates computed across sessions.
2. **Make retrieval the default, not an option.** Use cued recall, practise to criterion, and let the system initiate it.
3. **Externalise time, memory and starting.** Visual timers, a parking lot, preloaded first steps, and if-then next-start cues.
4. **Default to predictable, explicit, low-choice interfaces** that follow COGA. Put novelty in content variety, not in UI changes. This resolves the AuDHD novelty/sameness tension in a sensible direction.
5. **Remove shame mechanics:** no streaks, loss-framing, leaderboards, hard timeouts or notification spam.
6. **Treat noise, body doubling, movement primers and timer styles as personal N-of-1 experiments.** Log condition and outcome (retention at later review, not just in-session feel) and let the system recommend what works for this learner.
7. **Include a burnout guard** that shifts to maintenance mode on sustained decline.

## Caveats

- Several figures were obtained from secondary summaries rather than primary full texts and should be checked before being relied on. These include the body-doubling bricks-per-minute values, the Hupfeld 2024 r = 0.53, the RSD scoping-review counts and the d ≈ 0.43 discounting effect.
- Areas the brief requested but this review could not source directly are marked as gaps rather than filled with assumptions: sleep, sluggish cognitive tempo/cognitive disengagement syndrome, MOOCs, Autism&Uni and UK/EU higher-education guidance, interoception, and specific spacing/interleaving trials in ADHD.
- General learning-science claims (spacing, worked examples, learning styles) are drawn from the broad non-ADHD literature and are labelled as such.
- Sources are identified inline by author, year and venue throughout so each claim can be traced.
